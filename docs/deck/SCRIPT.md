# What to say, slide by slide

Five minutes, then two minutes of questions. About 560 words: 4:20 at an easy pace. The pitch may be given in
Kazakh, Russian or English; the deck stays in English. Update the test count if it changed.

**1. Practice tonight. Use it tomorrow.**
Your four-year-old holds up her blocks and says, "I made a tower!" What do you say back? Most of us say, "Good job!
What is it?" In a clinic, that scores zero. We're Fammy: a flight simulator for parents. Track: Skills.

**2. She has saved every script. She still says "Stop it."**
It's 11 PM. Jess, thirty-four, a nurse in Ohio, yelled at bedtime again. Her son Noah is four and melts down every
day. She has saved every script on Instagram. Knowing what to say isn't her problem. Saying it without thinking is.
Clinics don't start with discipline. They start with five calm minutes of play a day. That's a skill, and skills
need reps.

**3. Parents already pay for advice. Practice with a score is still stuck in clinics.**
Why now? Parents already pay: Good Inside made thirty-four million dollars last year selling videos and scripts. But
the method that actually trains the skill, PCIT, takes twelve to twenty weekly clinic sessions, often after months of
waiting. Advice is everywhere. Practice with a score is still stuck in the clinic.

**4. Before, a 30-second lesson, after on a new scene.**
Does it work? Here is one real run. Mia, a simulated four-year-old, talks first, and I answer in my own words. Every
sentence is coded against the numbers a clinic uses; they are on the slide. Thirty seconds of lesson, a second round
on a new scene, and this is my own before and after.

**5. No language model. Rules you can argue with.**
Under the hood there is no language model. Rules code every sentence, nearly two thousand tests hold them
together, and on eighty typed sentences it had never seen, it got ninety percent right. If you disagree with a code,
you tap Disagree. And a session costs us zero.

**6. Everyone tells parents what to say. We let them practice it, with a score.**
Is it new? Content brands tell you what to say. Chatbots can play a child, but they don't keep score. Clinics, and
Happypillar, now owned by a clinic network, score you only with your child in the room. Therapists can certify on
these same numbers in a five-minute role-play with an adult playing the child. We give parents that role-play.

**7. You pay for five coached minutes a day. You renew until you hit the numbers with your own child.**
What do we sell? Five coached minutes a day, twenty-nine dollars every four weeks. Weeks one to four are practice
with Mia, one skill a week. Day twenty-eight scores you against the clinic numbers. Why renew? The goal isn't day
twenty-eight. It's those numbers with your own child, so weeks five to eight move the practice into live mode.
Renewal isn't measured yet.

**8. The ad lands on the product. Break-even is 1.35% click-to-pay.**
How does it make money? The ad lands on Mia, not on a quiz, and the paywall comes after the parent's first win. With
no AI bill, margin before ads is ninety percent. A payer is worth eighty-nine dollars in a year, so we break even
when one point three five percent of clicks pay. The one benchmark we found is one point one. Nothing is measured
yet; that's our first test.

**9. Beachhead: 1.3M US homes with daily tantrums. $100M takes three steps.**
How big? Our beachhead is one point three million American homes with daily tantrums. Four percent of them is a
seventeen-million-dollar business. A hundred million needs more than tantrums: older kids, Spanish, a second
caregiver, then the same engine for other hard conversations. That is a ladder, not a promise.

**10. Open it. No sign-up. Three minutes.**
The link is live and needs no sign-up. The jury page lists what is real, what is simulated, and ways to break it. We
have zero users so far, and we say so. Next time a four-year-old says, "I made a tower," you'll know what to say.
Fammy: practice tonight, use it tomorrow.

## Do not say

"Nobody does this." "Ends tantrums." "Clinically proven." "AI therapist." The 0.87 effect size as our own. "Base
case 2.0%" (say "target"). "Works end to end on a phone" unless it was checked today. "Everything is live" unless
the link and the test payment passed today.

If the AI summary is switched on in production, slide 5 changes to "no language model in the scoring", and the
answer about audio changes to "recordings stay on the device and are off by default".
